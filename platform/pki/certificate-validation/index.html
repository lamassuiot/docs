Certificate validation

Check chain, identity, purpose and revocation from the consumer.

This guide brings together the checks needed when integrating a certificate consumer. You need the presented certificate, intermediates and roots that your application has deliberately chosen to trust. Lamassu publishes status; the consumer applies acceptance policy.

Before querying status

Verify these criteria together:

CheckRequired result
Chain and signatureValid chain to a root deliberately installed in the consumer.
DatesCurrent time between Not Before and Not After, including the CAs in the chain.
IdentityName, SAN or identifier matches the expected peer.
PurposeKU, EKU and constraints permit the specific operation.
RevocationSigned, current evidence corresponding to the queried issuer and certificate.
AuthorizationThe application permits that identity to perform the action.

The issuance walkthrough checks chain and purpose with OpenSSL. That check does not automatically query OCSP or download a CRL. Inventory presence or an OCSP Good response does not replace the other criteria.

Choose a mechanism

MechanismWhen to use itWhat you must maintain
OCSPStatus query for a specific certificate.Responder access, signature verification and cache/freshness policy.
CRLLocal checks for many certificates or offline operation.Periodic download of an issuer-signed list and rejection of expired lists.

OCSP can also return a response reusable until nextUpdate; a CRL changes when a new version is published. Neither mechanism alone guarantees that a consumer learns a revocation at the moment it is saved.

If using both, define when fallback is allowed and what the client does when retrieval, signature or freshness checks fail, or the result is Unknown. Accepting an identity when its status cannot be checked must be an explicit application decision.

Check consumer behavior

  1. Accept a current, authorized certificate with a known chain.
  2. Reject another certificate with an unknown root or incompatible purpose.
  3. Revoke a test certificate and verify the OCSP response or new CRL.
  4. Check that a new connection with that identity is rejected.
  5. Simulate an unreachable endpoint and expired evidence; observe whether the result matches policy.

Keep the serial number, issuer, revocation reason, query time and evidence timestamps. An existing session may need explicit closure or reauthentication in the consumer; publishing revocation does not automatically close it.

For a chain with intermediates, validate their status according to consumer policy as well. An end-entity issuer’s CRL does not replace checks on every CA in the chain.
